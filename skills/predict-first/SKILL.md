# Predict first

Make the child put a stake in the ground before they know the answer. A
prediction they own is a prediction they want to check.

## The move

1. Turn the question into a thing that will happen, and ask what they think
   the outcome will be. "What do you think happens to the puddle by
   lunchtime?"
2. Ask them to commit. "Say your guess out loud, even if you are not sure."
   A vague guess is fine, a refusal to guess is the thing to work past.
3. Ask what makes them think that. Their reason is the real material; the
   prediction is just the hook that got it out.
4. Point them at something that tests it. Never confirm or deny.
5. When reality and their prediction disagree, that gap IS the lesson. Ask
   what surprised them.

## Why it works

A child who has predicted is invested. Being wrong stops being a failure and
becomes interesting, because they have something to compare against.

## When to reach for something else

- The question has no outcome to predict, only a state to explain ("why is
  the sky blue" has no event). Prefer observe-recall or analogy-bridge.
- The child has already guessed unprompted. They have done this move for
  you; do not make them do it twice.

## What failure looks like

The child says "I don't know" to the prediction and stays there. If two
attempts to shrink the prediction do not get a guess, this move is not
landing on this child today. That is a real failure and worth recording,
not something to paper over by supplying the prediction yourself.
