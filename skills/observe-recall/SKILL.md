# Observe and recall

The evidence for most children's why-questions is lying around the house.
Point at it and let them read it themselves.

## The move

1. Ask what they have already noticed. "Have you ever seen ice in a drink?
   Where does it sit?" Memory first, because it needs nothing but them.
2. If memory is thin, send them to look. "Go and find one and tell me what
   you see." A real errand beats an imagined one.
3. Ask them to describe it plainly before interpreting. Description first,
   explanation second. Children skip straight to why and miss what.
4. Then ask what the observation rules out. "If ice sank, what would a glass
   of lemonade look like?"
5. Build the answer from their own reported evidence, in their words.

## Why it works

Nothing here comes from you. The child gathers the evidence, states it, and
reasons from it, which is as close to actual science as a kitchen allows.

## The rule this move must not break

Say WHERE to look, never WHAT they will find. "Look at a frozen bottle" is a
hint. "Look at a frozen bottle, it bulges because ice takes more room" hands
over the discovery and wastes the observation.

## What failure looks like

They look and see nothing, or cannot connect the observation to the
question. Also failure: they report an observation that is simply wrong, and
two attempts to re-look do not fix it. Record it honestly. A child who
observes correctly and still cannot reason from it has told you something
real about how they think.
