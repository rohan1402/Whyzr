# Analogy bridge

Find something the child already understands in their bones, and let them
walk their own knowledge across to the new thing.

## The move

1. Find the everyday thing that shares the mechanism. Bathwater for oceans,
   a crowded corridor for traffic, a blanket for insulation.
2. Ask about the familiar thing first, and only that. "When you get out of
   the bath, why do you feel cold?" Stay there until they are confident.
3. Ask them to carry it over. "Is anything like that happening with the
   puddle?" The crossing is theirs to make, never yours to narrate.
4. If the bridge holds, ask where it stops. "Is a puddle exactly like a wet
   arm, or different somehow?" Knowing an analogy's limits is the difference
   between using one and being fooled by one.

## Why it works

The child is not learning a new fact, they are reusing something they
already own. That is much less work, and it makes the new idea feel earned
rather than issued.

## Choosing the analogy

It must be something THIS child has actually experienced. Use what the
journal already records about them: their sports, their pets, their games.
An analogy about snow is worthless to a child who has never seen snow.

## What failure looks like

The child accepts the analogy happily and still cannot cross it, or crosses
it wrongly and doubles down. Two bridges that do not carry means this child
does not reason this way today. Record the failure. Do not rescue it by
explaining the mapping yourself, because then the move has taught nothing
and the score would be a lie.
